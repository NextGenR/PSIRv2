## 🌟 Overview

**PSIRv2** transforms the complete 77-page Sarrthi IAS PSIR Class Notes into a high-retention, interactive digital learning environment. Every static bullet point is replaced with a 3-choice micro-evaluation system, coupled with immediate positive reinforcement (flower petal showers and harmonic audio feedback).

## 📚 Complete Syllabus Coverage (All 6 Chapters • 77 Pages)

1. **Chapter 1: Political Ideologies** *(PDF pp. 3–8 • 49 Points)*
   - Nature, origin, and cognitive functions of Ideology (Michael Freeden, Destutt de Tracy).
   - Marxist perspective on Ideology & Base-Superstructure model.
   - **Core Marxist Concepts**: Ideology as Class Domination, False Consciousness, Class Consciousness & Revolution (Lukács), End of Ideology.
   - Expanded Views: Engels, Gramscian Cultural Hegemony, Althusser (ISAs), Marcuse, Lenin, Lukács, Mannheim.
   - Conservative critique of ideology: Michael Oakeshott.
   - Ideology and Totalitarianism: Hannah Arendt (*Origins of Totalitarianism*, Banality of Evil) & Karl Popper (*Open Society and Its Enemies*, Paradox of Tolerance).
   - "End of Ideology" Debate: Daniel Bell, Lipset, Fukuyama & critics (Marcuse, Mills, MacIntyre, Harrington).
   - 21st-Century Resurgence of Ideology.

2. **Chapter 2: Liberalism & Theories of the State** *(PDF pp. 9–18 • 113 Points)*
   - Core Liberal values: Individualism, Liberty, Rationality, Toleration, Constitutionalism.
   - Philosophical foundations: John Locke (Natural Rights, Social Contract) & Immanuel Kant (Categorical Imperative, Moral Autonomy).
   - **Classical Liberalism**: Negative Liberty, Night-Watchman minimal state, Adam Smith (*Wealth of Nations*), Herbert Spencer, A.V. Dicey (*Rule of Law*).
   - **Modern / Welfare Liberalism**: Positive Liberty, Enabling State, Social Justice, T.H. Green, L.T. Hobhouse, J.S. Mill (*On Liberty*, Harm Principle), J.M. Keynes, Amartya Sen (*Capability Approach*).
   - **Neo-Liberalism & The State**: Roll-back of the state, Market fundamentalism, F.A. Hayek (*Road to Serfdom*), Milton Friedman (*Free to Choose*), Robert Nozick (*Anarchy, State, and Utopia*, Entitlement Theory), Washington Consensus, Thatcherism & Reaganomics.
   - Critiques of Neo-Liberalism: Thomas Piketty, Joseph Stiglitz, David Harvey.
   - Isaiah Berlin: Value Pluralism and Two Concepts of Liberty.

3. **Chapter 3: Marxism, Neo-Marxism & State Theories** *(PDF pp. 19–40 • 115 Points)*
   - Dialectical and Historical Materialism.
   - Labor Theory of Value & Surplus Value.
   - Marx’s Four-Fold Theory of Alienation (product, process, species-essence, fellow humans).
   - Class Struggle & Revolutionary Transformation (Dictatorship of Proletariat, Withering Away of the State).
   - **Theories of the State**:
     - **Instrumentalist State**: Ralph Miliband (*The State in Capitalist Society*).
     - **Structuralist State**: Nicos Poulantzas (*Political Power and Social Classes*, Relative Autonomy of the State), Louis Althusser (ISAs vs. RSAs).
     - **The Miliband–Poulantzas Debate**: Agency vs. Structural determination.
   - Marxist Internationalism & Working-Class Solidarity.
   - Leninism (Vanguard Party, Democratic Centralism, Imperialism as Highest Stage of Capitalism).
   - Maoism (Peasantry, New Democracy, Cultural Revolution, Mass Line).
   - Antonio Gramsci: Cultural Hegemony, Organic Intellectuals, War of Position vs. War of Movement.
   - Frankfurt School & Critical Theory: Adorno, Horkheimer (*Dialectic of Enlightenment*), Herbert Marcuse (*One-Dimensional Man*), Jürgen Habermas (*Communicative Action*).
   - Contemporary Marxism: Eco-Marxism (John Bellamy Foster), Feminist Marxism (Silvia Federici), Queer Marxism, African Marxism (Frantz Fanon).

4. **Chapter 4: Socialism and Fascism** *(PDF pp. 40–55 • 256 Points)*
   - Origin & Evolution of Socialism.
   - Utopian Socialists: Robert Owen (New Lanark), Charles Fourier (Phalansteries), Henri de Saint-Simon.
   - Scientific Socialism (Marx & Engels).
   - Evolutionary Socialism & Revisionism (Eduard Bernstein).
   - Fabian Socialism (Sidney & Beatrice Webb, George Bernard Shaw, Clement Attlee).
   - Guild Socialism & Syndicalism (G.D.H. Cole, Arthur Penty).
   - Equality under Socialism & Democratic Socialism in the 21st century.
   - **Fascism**:
     - Nature, origins, and intellectual roots (Nietzsche, Georges Sorel, Social Darwinism).
     - Totalitarian state ("Everything in the State, nothing outside the State, nothing against the State" – Mussolini).
     - Italian Fascism vs. German National Socialism (Hitler, Racial Supremacy, Lebensraum).
     - Contemporary far-right extremism and digital radicalization.

5. **Chapter 5: Gandhism** *(PDF pp. 56–69 • 241 Points)*
   - Life, spiritualized politics, and influences (Tolstoy, Ruskin, Thoreau, Bhagavad Gita).
   - Satya (Truth is God) & Ahimsa (Non-violence as creed of the brave).
   - Theory & Practice of Satyagraha (Soul-force, Non-cooperation, Civil disobedience, Fasting, Constructive Programme).
   - Swaraj: Four dimensions (Political independence, Economic Swadeshi, Social equality, Spiritual self-rule).
   - Sarvodaya: Welfare of All (*Unto This Last*).
   - Theory of Trusteeship (Wealth with moral responsibility).
   - Gandhian Stateless Democracy & Oceanic Circles: Critique of sovereign coercive state, enlightened anarchy, Village Swaraj (Panchayati Raj).
   - *Hind Swaraj* (1909): Critique of modern Western industrial civilization, railways, lawyers, doctors.
   - Gandhi on Caste, Untouchability (*Harijan*), and Women's Emancipation.
   - Contemporary relevance of Gandhi (Civil rights, Chipko movement, E.F. Schumacher's *Small is Beautiful*).

6. **Chapter 6: Feminism** *(PDF pp. 69–76 • 78 Points)*
   - Definition & Distinction between Sex and Gender.
   - Four Waves of Feminism:
     - First Wave (Suffrage, legal equality, Wollstonecraft, Seneca Falls, Anthony, Pankhurst).
     - Second Wave ("The Personal is Political", Betty Friedan, Simone de Beauvoir, reproductive rights, Roe v. Wade).
     - Third Wave (Intersectionality, Judith Butler, bell hooks, Riot Grrrl).
     - Fourth Wave (Digital activism, #MeToo, bodily autonomy, consent).
   - Liberal Feminism: Legal reform, equal opportunities (Wollstonecraft, Mill, Friedan).
   - Radical Feminism: Patriarchy as fundamental root oppression, sex-class (Shulamith Firestone, Kate Millett *Sexual Politics*).
   - Socialist & Marxist Feminism: Capitalism and patriarchy as dual systems, domestic labor exploitation (Sylvia Walby, Sheila Rowbotham).
   - Black & Intersectional Feminism: Kimberlé Crenshaw, bell hooks, Audre Lorde.
   - Ecofeminism: Vandana Shiva, connection between exploitation of women and nature.
   - Contemporary Global & Transnational Feminism.

## ⚡ Core Interactive Features

- **3-Choice Micro-Evaluator**:
  - `✓` (Right Sign - Green): Mastered
  - `✕` (Wrong Sign - Red): Needs Work / Priority Revision
  - `○` (Circle Sign - Yellow): In Progress / Flagged
- **Flower Petal Shower Engine**: Realistic floating petals triggered upon every `✓` evaluation.
- **Harmonic Audio Chimes**: Web Audio API generated synthesizer chord progressions.
- **Core Marxist Concepts Milestone**: Automatically detects when all 4 core concepts on Page 3 are marked `✓` and displays the grand celebration banner:
  > **`🎉 CORE-MARXIST CONCEPTS COMPLETED! 100% Retained`**
- **Collapsible Table of Contents (Index)**: Full hierarchical drawer for quick navigation.
- **Universal Live Search**: Instant full-text search across all 852 points.
- **Filter Bar**: View filtered subsets (`All`, `Mastered ✓`, `Needs Work ✕`, `Flagged ○`, `Unmarked`).
- **Dark & Light Paper Themes**: Toggle between high-contrast light paper and sleek dark study mode.
- **Auto-Persistence**: All evaluation marks save locally to browser `localStorage`.

## 🚀 How to Run Locally

1. Clone the repository:
   ```bash
   git clone https://github.com/NextGenR/PSIRv2.git
   cd PSIRv2
   ```

2. Start a local server:
   ```bash
   # Using Python
   python -m http.server 5500
   
   # Or using Node
   npx serve .
   ```

3. Open in your browser:
   ```
   http://localhost:5500/index.html
   ```
